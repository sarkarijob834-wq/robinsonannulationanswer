Wieland–Miescher ketone and Hajos–Parrish ketone

Updated 2026-10-19

Key takeaways
  • Both are bicyclic enones made by Robinson-type annulation.
  • Wieland–Miescher uses a six-membered dione; Hajos–Parrish a five-membered one.
  • Proline catalysis gives high enantioselectivity.

These two bicyclic ketones are among the most cited examples of the Robinson annulation. They are useful building blocks for steroids and terpenoids.

Wieland–Miescher ketone

It forms from 2-methylcyclohexane-1,3-dione and methyl vinyl ketone. A Michael addition attaches a 3-oxobutyl chain, an intramolecular aldol closes a second six-membered ring, and dehydration gives the enone.

Hajos–Parrish ketone

The five-membered analogue starts from 2-methylcyclopentane-1,3-dione. The sequence is the same, but the product has a fused five- and six-membered ring system.

Why proline matters

The diketone starting material is symmetrical, but the aldol step can break that symmetry. L-Proline acts as an organocatalyst and enables the ring closure with high enantioselectivity, which is why these reactions are a classic example of asymmetric organocatalysis.

What to remember

  • Name the starting dione and the acceptor.
  • Draw the Michael adduct, then the aldol, then the enone.
  • Note that proline gives one enantiomer predominantly.
