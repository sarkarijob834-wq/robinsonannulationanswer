Robinson annulation mechanism explained step by step

Updated 2026-10-19

Key takeaways
  • The mechanism has three stages: Michael addition, intramolecular aldol, dehydration.
  • The Michael product is a 1,5-diketone.
  • The final product is a conjugated cyclohexenone.

If you can draw the mechanism cleanly, you can answer almost any Robinson annulation question. The trick is to treat it as three small reactions you already know, linked together. This guide walks through them in the order you should draw them in an exam.

Step 0: pick the donor and acceptor

The donor is a ketone with an acidic α-hydrogen. The acceptor is an α,β-unsaturated ketone, usually methyl vinyl ketone (MVK). Label the donor's α-carbon and the acceptor's β-carbon before you draw anything, because those two atoms form the first new bond.

Step 1: form the enolate

Hydroxide or alkoxide removes an α-hydrogen from the donor. Draw the arrow from the base to the hydrogen, then from the C–H bond into the carbon–carbon bond to make the enolate. If the ketone is unsymmetrical, note which side you chose and why.

Step 2: Michael addition

The enolate carbon attacks the β-carbon of MVK, pushing the C=C electrons onto the oxygen as a new enolate. Protonation gives a 1,5-diketone. A quick check: count the carbons between the two carbonyls. There should be three.

Step 3: intramolecular aldol

Base forms a second enolate. Choose the α-carbon that is five atoms away from the carbonyl it will attack, so the ring contains six atoms in total. The enolate adds to that carbonyl, giving a cyclic β-hydroxy ketone.

Step 4: dehydration

Base removes the remaining α-hydrogen and hydroxide leaves (E1cB). The new C=C sits next to the carbonyl, so it is conjugated. That conjugation is the thermodynamic reason the reaction goes all the way to the enone.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Stopping at the aldol alcohol.
  • Forming a four- or five-membered ring by picking the wrong enolate.
  • Placing the double bond away from the carbonyl.
